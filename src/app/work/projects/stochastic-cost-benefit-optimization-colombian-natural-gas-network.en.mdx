## Overview

This project develops a **stochastic cost-benefit optimization** framework for the **Colombian natural gas network**, but its foundation is a much broader data and modeling effort: reconstructing the network physically, transforming historical incidents into usable labels, and generating the analytical base required for failure forecasting and risk-aware planning.

The work addresses critical gaps in current planning by the Energy and Mining Planning Unit (UPME), where deterministic representations often understate **stochastic dynamics**, **topographic realism**, and the simultaneous uncertainty affecting supply and transport infrastructure.

## Methodology

The proposed approach integrates **network reconstruction**, **geospatial feature engineering**, **LLM-assisted NLP**, and **stochastic simulation** into one decision-support pipeline.

Key components include:

- **System Representation:** Rebuilt a segment-level georeferenced data product for Colombia&apos;s national transmission system, aligning simplified line connections with physical corridors and elevation profiles.
- **Feature Pipeline:** Engineered a modeling base with approximately **32,000 observations** and **80+ static and temporal variables** spanning topographic, seismic, climatic, and societal risk factors.
- **Incident Intelligence:** Built an **LLM-assisted NLP workflow** to clean, standardize, and classify **20 years of Spanish-language failure reports** by cause, replacing manual categorization with scalable processing.
- **Analytical Base:** Delivered a **length-normalized segment-year database** to support downstream survival analysis and machine-learning failure-rate forecasting.
- **Decision Support:** Re-evaluated planning scenarios under **Monte Carlo N-1 simulations** to assess operational risk, shortage costs, and transport-capacity decisions under uncertainty.

## Outcome

The result is a planning framework that connects infrastructure realism with data-driven forecasting, making it possible to compare deterministic and stochastic decisions using a technically coherent representation of the network.

In practice, the project supports **reliability assessment**, **failure prioritization**, and **energy-security planning** by turning fragmented infrastructure and incident data into a forecasting-ready system for operators, researchers, and policy stakeholders.
